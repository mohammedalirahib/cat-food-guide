✅ Safe Foods - Detailed Information

General Rule: Always cook foods without seasoning, salt, or oil. Remove bones and serve in small portions.
  • Chicken & Turkey: Excellent source of lean protein. Must be cooked plain without seasoning.
  • Fish: Safe in moderation. Avoid raw fish and remove all bones. High in omega-3 fatty acids.
  • Eggs: Cooked eggs are nutritious. Never give raw eggs due to salmonella risk.
  • Pumpkin: Great for digestive health. Use plain canned pumpkin, not pie filling.
  • Vegetables: Carrots, green beans, and peas are safe when cooked and chopped small.
  • Watermelon: Remove seeds and rind. Offer small pieces as occasional treats.
  • Cooked Rice: Plain white or brown rice aids digestion.
  • Plain Yogurt: Small amounts are okay, but many cats are lactose intolerant.

❌ Harmful Foods - Why They're Dangerous

⚠️ URGENT: If your cat ingests any toxic food, contact your veterinarian or animal poison control immediately!
  • Chocolate: Contains theobromine and caffeine. Dark chocolate is most dangerous. Even small amounts can cause vomiting and heart issues.
  • Onions & Garlic: Destroy hemoglobin in red blood cells, causing anemia. Cooked or raw, both are toxic.
  • Grapes & Raisins: Cause acute kidney failure. The exact toxic compound is unknown, but even small amounts are dangerous.
  • Avocado: Contains persin, a fungicidal toxin that can cause vomiting and diarrhea.
  • Alcohol: Causes intoxication, low blood sugar, and respiratory depression. Can be fatal.
  • Caffeine: Found in coffee, tea, and energy drinks. Causes increased heart rate and tremors.
  • Xylitol (Artificial Sweetener): Found in sugar-free products. Causes rapid insulin release and liver damage.
  • Milk & Dairy: Many adult cats are lactose intolerant and experience digestive issues.
  • Raw Dough: Can expand in the stomach and cause bloating or alcohol production.
  • Bones: Can splinter and cause choking or internal injuries.

🚨 Emergency Tips

  • Poison Control: ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center: (888) 426-4435
  • Symptoms of Poisoning: Vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy, drooling, difficulty breathing, or seizures
  • Action Steps: Don't wait for symptoms. Call immediately if your cat ate something toxic.
  • Prevention: Store all human food securely and never leave toxic foods unattended on counters or tables.
  • Know Your Cat's Diet: Keep a record of your cat's normal diet and any foods given as treats.
